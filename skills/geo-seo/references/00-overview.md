# 00. Overview: being recommended by Google, Bing and AI assistants

This file sets the framework: what GEO is, through which channels an assistant ends up naming a site, what
really matters (in what order), who the robots are, and which myths to discard. The detailed verdicts with
sources are in `01-research-verdicts.md`. The worked example is the campaign run in October 2026 on
jam-jam.org (static Astro site, 10 languages, about 730 indexable pages).

Last check of the cited sources: 14 October 2026. What could not be verified is stated as such.

## 1. What GEO means

- **SEO**: being ranked by search engines (Google, Bing).
- **GEO** (Generative Engine Optimization) and **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimization): being cited or recommended
  in an answer generated by an AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, AI Overviews, DeepSeek).

Google's official position, published on 15 May 2026 in its guide to optimizing for generative AI search
features: "from the point of view of Google Search, optimizing for generative search is optimizing for the
search experience, so it is still SEO". AI Overviews and AI Mode rely on the ordinary ranking and index
(page retrieval, plus an expansion of the query into sub-queries, called "query fan-out"). This position
applies to Google; for the other assistants, the evidence is thinner (see `01`), but the practical conclusion
is the same: a site that is fast to crawl, clear, factual and cited elsewhere has the best chance everywhere.

Methodological consequence: serious GEO is solid SEO, plus three things specific to assistants (access for AI
robots, self-contained and citable passages, off-site presence). There is no magic tag.

## 2. The three routes by which an assistant recommends a site

| Route | Mechanism | Delay | What feeds it | What we control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Model memory | The model "read" the site (or what is said about it) during training | Months, sometimes a year | GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Meta-ExternalAgent, open corpora, and everything written about the brand elsewhere | Little: allow training robots, be mentioned elsewhere |
| 2. Live search | The assistant runs a search, retrieves page passages, cites them | Days to weeks (indexing) | Google index (AI Overviews, AI Mode, Gemini), Bing (Copilot, and according to some reports ChatGPT), own indexes (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot) and on-demand visits (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User) | A lot: indexing, access, citable content |
| 3. Entity | The assistant knows that "X" is a given software/organization/person, with a given name, URL and platforms | Weeks to months | Google Knowledge Graph, Wikidata, consistent profiles (`sameAs`), About page, mentions | Moderate: clear identity, real profiles, linked JSON-LD |

Points that structure everything else:

1. **Assistants retrieve passages, not pages.** Each section must be understandable on its own (topic named in
   the section heading, answer in the first lines, numbers with their unit). Google says that deliberately
   splitting the text is not necessary for it; it is simply good writing, useful for the other engines and at
   no cost.
2. **The HTML served is what counts.** Measurement published in December 2024 (Vercel and a rendering analysis
   firm): the robots of OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, ByteDance and Perplexity, as well as CCBot, do not execute
   JavaScript; Googlebot (therefore Gemini and AI Overviews) and Applebot do. A site that builds its text
   client-side is invisible to part of the assistants. On jam-jam.org, the `/app/*` SPA is deliberately excluded
   from search and each tool has an indexable static "twin".
3. **External mentions count more than tags** (see §5 and `06-off-site.md`).
4. **Answers vary from one run to the next.** Every measurement must be repeated (see `07-measurement.md`).

## 3. What really matters, in what order (impact per cost)

Order adopted in the jam-jam.org campaign, justified in `01`:

| Rank | Lever | Why | Cost | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One URL per page (trailing slash, internal links without redirects) | Signals are no longer split between two addresses; crawl savings | Low (one helper + one blocking check) | Google documentation (duplicate content); local measurement: `/accordeur-basse` position 62.7 versus `/accordeur-basse/` position 40.7 |
| 2 | Reliable sitemap: real `lastmod`, everything declared, submitted | The only official channel for signaling changes to Google; IndexNow for Bing | Low | Google documentation (`lastmod` is used if verifiably accurate; `priority` and `changefreq` are ignored) |
| 3 | Access: robots.txt with no accidental blocking, no WAF refusing AI robots | A blocked robot never cites | Low | OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity documentation |
| 4 | Titles and descriptions rewritten for queries already in positions 5 to 20 | Immediate click gain (Google and AI Overviews) | Low to medium | The site's Search Console data |
| 5 | Answer block at the top, verified figures, self-contained sections | Citable passages; facts and sources increase visibility | Medium | Princeton GEO study (KDD 2024), probable |
| 6 | Entity: JSON-LD linked by `@id`, real `sameAs`, real publisher and author, About page | Remove ambiguity (homonyms), feed the knowledge graph | Medium | Google documentation (Organization), practice |
| 7 | Pages for needs that have no page, citable reference articles | A query with no page has no chance | High | The site's Search Console data |
| 8 | Off-site presence (directories, YouTube, GitHub, press, genuine reviews) | Strongest correlation with AI visibility; it is the ceiling of the site alone | High, manual | Ahrefs studies (correlations, not causation) |
| 9 | Measurement: Search Console, "AI assistants" channel, question panel | Without measurement, we do not know whether it helped | Medium | Method |
| 10 | llms.txt | Almost no value for visibility, but free if generated at build time | Low | Ahrefs (137,000 domains), Google |

Decision rule: as long as ranks 1 to 3 are not green, write nothing new. Excellent content behind a silent
sitemap, duplicate URLs or a blocked robot earns nothing.

## 4. Robots and engines

### 4.1 Three roles not to be confused

Each major player now separates its robots into three roles, which can be controlled independently in
robots.txt:

- **Training**: collect content for future models (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended,
  Applebot-Extended...).
- **Search index**: build the index that feeds answers with citations (OAI-SearchBot,
  Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Bingbot, Googlebot).
- **On-demand visit**: go and read a page because a user asked for it (ChatGPT-User,
  Claude-User, Perplexity-User, MistralAI-User). Several of these robots state that robots.txt may not
  apply, since the action comes from a human.

To be cited, the last two roles must be allowed. Blocking only training is a legitimate choice, with no effect
on search at OpenAI ("each setting is independent of the others", OpenAI documentation). In the campaign, all
of them are allowed: the goal is recommendation.

Practical remarks:

- A robot not named in robots.txt applies the `*` group. Naming each robot mainly makes the intent explicit and
  guards against a CDN that would inject its own groups.
- Access test: AI robots are verified by user-agent **and** by IP address (publishers publish their ranges). A
  `curl` with a fake user-agent only proves the user-agent rule (see `02`).
- Cloudflare announced in July 2025 that it would block AI robots by default, which the owner can lift
  (company announcement). If the site is behind a CDN or WAF, check its AI robot settings and its user-agent
  rules, not only robots.txt.

### 4.3 Which engine feeds which assistant

| Assistant | What feeds it | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews, AI Mode | Google index, page retrieval (RAG) and query fan-out | Google doc |
| Gemini (application) | Google index for "grounding"; Google-Extended governs grounding and training | Google doc |
| Microsoft Copilot | Bing (Copilot Search in Bing) | Microsoft doc |
| ChatGPT (search) | OAI-SearchBot (own index) plus third-party search providers; Bing widely cited as a provider | OpenAI does not detail; help page unreachable without JavaScript during verification: not confirmed directly |
| Perplexity | PerplexityBot plus other undocumented sources | Partial |
| Claude | Claude-SearchBot and Claude-User; external search provider not documented (often attributed to Brave Search) | Not verified |
| DuckDuckGo (AI answers) | DuckAssistBot and own index | DuckDuckGo doc |
| Siri, Spotlight | Applebot | Apple doc |
| Mistral | MistralAI-Index and MistralAI-User | Mistral doc |
| DeepSeek | Unknown | Not verified |

Strategic reading: a site well indexed by Google **and** Bing covers AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot and, very
probably, a good part of ChatGPT. This is why levers 1 to 3 of §3 come before everything else, and why IndexNow
submission (Bing) and Search Console (Google) are part of the deployment.

## 5. What counts off the site

Published correlations (correlation is not causation):

- Ahrefs, 75,000 brands: brand web mentions correlate at 0.664 with visibility in AI Overviews, inbound links at
  0.218.
- Ahrefs, late 2025: brand mentions on YouTube correlate at about 0.737, the best factor measured, ahead of web
  mentions (0.66 to 0.71).
- Semrush, 230,000 queries over 13 weeks: Wikipedia and Reddit dominate ChatGPT citations, but the shares move
  strongly from one month to the next (sharp drop in September 2025).

Google, for its part, warns against searching for "inauthentic" mentions. Keep in mind: **being genuinely
mentioned** (directories, comparison articles, videos, public repositories, genuine reviews) helps; fabricating
mentions harms (see myths). The operational detail is in `06-off-site.md`. On jam-jam.org, the audit found three
known external mentions and 72 brand impressions over 90 days: that is the site's real ceiling.

## 6. Myths to discard

1. "Tags (JSON-LD, schema) serve AIs on a silver platter." Google: no special markup is required; ChatGPT and
   Perplexity read an invalid JSON-LD as plain text in a published test. Useful for Google (entity, rich
   results) and Bing, not an AI lever.
2. "You need an llms.txt." Google ignores it; across 137,000 domains, 97% of the files received no request in
   May 2026 (Ahrefs). Free to generate, do not invest in it.
3. "The first result on Google will be cited by ChatGPT." False: ChatGPT has its own index and sources; even for
   AI Overviews, only 38% of cited URLs are in the top 10 for the same query (Ahrefs, March 2026, against 76% in
   July 2025).
4. "Blocking Google-Extended removes you from AI Overviews." False: it has no effect on Search.
5. "Blocking GPTBot removes you from ChatGPT." False: GPTBot is training; search depends on OAI-SearchBot.
6. "FAQs in JSON-LD give rich results." Not at all any more: restricted in August 2023 to official health and
   government sites, then removed on 7 May 2026. The **visible** FAQ block remains useful to the reader.
7. "The Indexing API speeds up indexing of all pages." Reserved for job postings and live broadcasts; risk of
   revocation in case of abuse.
8. "IndexNow alerts Google." No: only Bing, Yandex, Naver, Seznam, Yep and Amazon.
9. "Just write for AIs (hidden text, instructions to models)." Treated as cloaking; Google specifies that its
   anti-spam rules apply to generative answers.
10. "More pages, more visibility." Pages mass-produced for each query variant fall under "scaled content abuse";
    Google repeats this in its May 2026 guide.
11. "There is an ideal length or a magic format." Google: there is no ideal length.
12. "Wikipedia, Reddit: create a quick presence there." Throwaway accounts and self-promotional pages fail
    (notability criteria) and expose you to sanctions.

## 7. Honesty about measurement

- No third-party tool has access to Google's internal systems; be wary of "GEO scores".
- Pew Research (July 2025, 900 US adults): with an AI summary, 8% of visits click a classic result, against 15%
  without a summary; 1% of visits click a link in the summary. This corrects a common version of this figure ("8% click
  after an AI answer"), which mixes the two.
- Ahrefs: assistants often cite the brand without a link; a link appears in 10.7% of AI Overviews mentions and
  51.6% of Perplexity mentions. Count citations without links.
- On jam-jam.org, no effect is measured at the time of writing: the deployment dates from 10 October 2026, and
  panel readings are planned at 2 to 4 weeks, CTR targets at 4 to 8 weeks.
